# Treatment: Who Routes the Agents? (V2)

Three structurally different candidates were written on 2026-11-16 by three design agents, each
from one lens and from the same brief, facts matrix and benchmark study. Three judges then read all
three: an art director (who also looked at the benchmark's frames), a facts auditor (against
[FACTS.md](FACTS.md)) and a first-time viewer (who timed every line against the hold rule). The
user chose the hybrid the judges recommended. The candidates and verdicts in full are in the
session's research cache, not in git.

## Candidate A: the layer stack ("The Missing Layer")
- **Arc:** flat, you are the router → the lift: routing becomes its own layer → two levels → the
  worry, one Router for all this? → four answers along the stack (gates, threads, batching,
  cache) → evidence → built, proposed, open → you, one layer up.
- **Image system:** strata. Height is distance from the work: WORLD, WORKERS, ROUTER, YOU. One
  plane in focus. Light is a model thinking, so the light in frame reads as cost.
- **Sound:** the stack is a four-note chord; the drop opens a unison into the chord.
- **What could go wrong:** kinship with the benchmark's two tilted planes; a tall object in a wide
  frame foreshortens to slivers; six stops plus evidence and status turn reading-bound.

## Candidate B: follow one signal ("Follow the Signal")
- **Arc:** one event waits for you → you let go and it falls → sorted by code, then a model →
  routed, not worked → worked alone, follow-ups ride its thread → returned and recalled → signed
  off; pull out to the journey as one stroke among many → what changed for you.
- **Image system:** one unbroken take locked to a single dot falling through decks. A line is
  only history: the trail is the Thread.
- **Sound:** one pinged note for the signal; off-grid notes quantise into a chord for a batch; the
  motif resolves at the sign-off.
- **What could go wrong:** seven mechanisms under a moving camera; one signal implies one built
  pipeline, and the path it follows is off by default in the prototype; a lone dot reads as
  "too simple".

## Candidate C: typography-first argument ("You Are the Router")
- **Arc:** past three to five, lines stall → so you are the router → hand it down → what wakes it,
  who decides → how one Router keeps up, what it remembers → what it costs → built, proposed,
  open; you decide and sign off.
- **Image system:** a ruled page in depth. A rule is a context alive through time, a full stop an
  event, a caret your attention, a bracket one model call. Each question's "?" sheds its dot,
  which becomes the event that answers it.
- **Sound:** one tick per caret building five against four; each question opens on the same
  rising figure.
- **What could go wrong:** a slogan slideshow (text on screen about 80% of the time, V1's
  failure); five mark meanings to learn in 24 s; slogans compress qualified facts.

## Choice: the layer stack, cut on C's clock
**A is the body** because it is the only candidate whose picture is one organised, hierarchical
whole, which is what the user asked for and what V1 lacked. Its mechanisms form one argument, and
its light carries a quantity. **C gives the clock and the copy rules:** a question hook, the drop
at 0:16, lines that fit bars, hollow numerals, the batching bound. **B gives three devices:** a
line is only ever history, status is stamped at the mechanism it is true for, and the sign-off
resolves both the mark and the score's motif.

### The image
- **Strata.** From the bottom: WORLD (events happen there, drawn as lanes of dots, not a slab),
  the WORKERS slab, the ROUTER slab, and YOU (a coral dot and its attention, not a slab). The two
  slabs are the article's two levels, and the film says "two levels" once.
- **Flow.** A first event rises from its lane through gate one (plain code, a ruled edge directly
  above WORLD), then up an open shaft to the Router, whose own light judges it (gate two is the
  Router's model call). The Router hands the whole task down: a cell closes round it on the
  WORKERS slab, above the lane it came from. A follow-up rises from the same lane and stops at
  that cell; the Router registers an unlit tick. A result rises to YOU, and one coral touch signs
  it off.
- **Marks.** A dot is one event (bone raw, ash dropped, a coral ring when a person sent it). A
  bone-outlined capsule is a whole task. A closed cell is one session's context. A line is history
  or a subscription and is never drawn ahead of its explanation.
- **Light.** Phosphor light is a model inferring right now. Coral is your attention. Code, type
  and idle marks never glow, so brightness reads as token cost.
- **Status.** Solid is in the public prototype, dashed is proposed, a gap is open. Hollow numerals
  are figures from the authors' synthetic tests. Mono stamps in a hairline bracket sit on the
  plane edge at the mechanism they are true for.
- **Camera.** One rig with a continuous tilt and no cuts between modes. A steep look-down
  explains one mechanism on one plane, with the plane beneath showing through, defocused. A
  near edge-on view shows the strata for the lift, the hand-offs, the status and the recap.

### The arc (planned: about 2:40, 80 bars of 2 s at 120 bpm)

**As built (2026-11-17, after the review round).** The film is 84 bars, 2:48: evidence runs to
bar 66, the status frame is bars 66 to 70, the close bars 70 to 79, and the recap got four bars
of its own (76 to 79) before the end card (80 to 84). Departures from the lines above, each for a
reason found on frames:
- The recap is one list over the clear ground at your level, not on the spine: over the stack its
  edges ran through the words.
- The prototype stamp says "keeps a shell, no write tools", not "a restricted shell": the
  prototype's presets are not a sandbox ([FACTS.md](FACTS.md), section 11, `proto1`).
- Stamps stand without a hairline bracket.
- The name EVENTS stands under its lanes, below the line of the bottom 15%; no line of the cue
  sheet does.
- The session count of the opening is set solid, so that hollow numerals mean one thing.

About 51% of the runtime explains how the design works (V1: 43%). Left out, with reasons: the four
traits as a list (the benchmark's centrepiece), speculative execution (a test fixture in the
prototype, and the article's own example cannot be reused), the async loop and the 100-word cap
(channel behaviour with traps around defaults), sharding and BYOM beyond one word.

### Type
- Caps headlines up to about 280 px, grown from the active plane's edge with a per-glyph stagger
  and motion blur; entrances end on the beat. One-bar lines have at most 21 characters, two-bar
  blocks at most 51.
- Sentence-case support lines at 60 px or more, beside the active mark. Mono stamps at 52 px.
- No subtitle strip, nothing in the bottom 15% of the frame, nothing under 52 px that is meant to
  be read. Type is bone and never glows. Only YOU and ATTENTION are ever coral.
- Every mechanism opens with a bar without words.

### Look: phosphor and coral on pitch
A warm violet-black ground, bone type and structure, phosphor green only where a model is
thinking, coral only for you, ash for dropped and idle marks. No starfield and no grid. Finish:
edge-lit slabs with analytic defocus by distance from the focus plane, bloom on emissive marks
only, one additive spill sprite on the plane below a lit mark, sub-frame motion blur, dither and
fine static grain. Two alternative palettes are shown to the user as style frames at the pilot.

### Sound
120 bpm. The stack is a chord with one pitch per plane, and the bass moves to the visited plane's
note. The drop lands on the lift. Gate one is an unpitched click and a model call a pad swell. A
batch is off-grid notes resolving into one chord. Evidence runs half-time. The motif stays
incomplete until the sign-off.

### Guard rails (the judges' must-fixes)
- The active plane fills the frame; the stack never sits in a central band of slivers. If the
  tilted view fails that at lookdev, the layers hang face-on in depth as bands.
- Slabs are full-bleed and leave the frame on both sides, never floating outlined panels. Labels
  sit on the spine. The film opens on the trail, not a grid of cards.
- At most 7 lit marks, one headline and one support line. At most three one-off set pieces: the
  lift, the batching interval, the cache shelf. No tool slots, base tile or simulated rival.
- Kill test at lookdev: a context-free viewer is shown our stack frame and the benchmark's
  two-plane frame. If the answer is "same film, different colour", the staging changes before any
  build.
- Copy: baselines are named, "per decision" is on the readout, "by design" sits on what the
  prototype does not do as built, a prototype default sits beside the mechanism it qualifies, and
  the test caveats come before its numbers. One word, "events", throughout. Our own framings
  (the title question, "our reading") are marked as ours in the claims table.
- One illustrative request of our own carries "event" through gate, Router and Worker as a tracer.
  It is tagged illustrative and never shares a frame with the test's figures.

## Do not copy
- **The benchmark's signatures:** a navy ground with one electric-blue glow; session cards → dots
  → ring → orb → particle-burst title; "big word left, icon right" trait cards; a decorative
  starfield; a bottom subtitle line with coloured keywords; a wordmark with a hyphen pill; a
  slatted column and a circular lens for the two-stage filter; a dashed bypass under a central
  Router node; an odometer of minutes; two tilted outlined planes with a tool chip.
- **The article's own figures:** its hub-and-spoke diagram, its two-stage sieve, its swimlanes,
  its cloud-over-machines figure, its completion and batching charts, its examples and IDs.
- **V1:** the Register Desk, the four chores, the transit map, a node-and-link diagram as the
  governing image.
- **The genre:** gold on navy, serif caption bands, sparkle that means nothing.

## The critic's rubric for this film
The frame-critic's calibration rule says to score the user's references first and to revise the
rubric when a reference fails a starred item or a P0 tell. The benchmark does: it has glow on
type, particles and thin lines on a dark ground. For this film those tells become "glow, particles
or lines that carry no meaning", and the starred Material, Light and World-layers items are judged
as hierarchy, meaning of light and depth between planes. The upper anchor is the benchmark's
frames, side by side at 1080p. The lower anchor is the user's own words about V1 ("too many
detailed 3D objects scattered across a plane, competing for attention"); V1's stills are not
rebuilt, because its outputs are deleted and the brief names its failure. A pass is not
"competitive": every verdict also says how the frame stands beside the benchmark.

## How it is built
The web engine, one WebGL2 canvas drawn by the film's own small kit (no three.js: the film needs
only flat shapes, ribbons, glyph quads and post passes), 1920×1080 at 60 fps. Type is rasterised once on a
CPU canvas into a glyph atlas and drawn as quads in the scene, so words share its camera, bloom
and motion blur. All film code stays in this slot: `src/cues.ts` (the one timeline), `src/kit/`,
`src/scenes/`, `audio/`. This departs from decision 30 (a lit world under DOM text); ARCHITECTURE
decision 34 records why.

## The pilot's first review (2026-11-16)
Three reviewers looked at the first cut of bars 1 to 17, each without the others' notes.
- **Kill test: passed.** A viewer with no context compared our strata frame with the benchmark's
  two-plane frame, and our sessions frame with its card grid: "different films" both times
  (closeness 3 and 4 out of 10). What still echoes it: a user disc in a ring at the upper left,
  and outlined rounded cells.
- **Frame critic: partial.** The strata were hairlines and slivers, the action sat small under
  the headlines, and lines and marks crossed the type. Its fixes were applied: slabs have a body
  and carry their names on their front faces; the Router is a rail with three labelled channels
  and the task visibly drops to a large cell; slab edges no longer run behind the words; the
  count sits above the trail; coral is lighter than the green.
- **First-time viewer:** followed the story and called it "finished, not a rough diagram", with
  the tangle and its flattening the strongest images. Three lines were changed for them: the 3 to
  5 line names its source in one sentence, a stalled session now calls for you with a ring
  opening from it, and "IT DOESN'T WORK" (read as "it is broken") became "IT NEVER EXECUTES".
- **Not yet done:** a second critic pass after those fixes; "YOU DO." with the dot as its full
  stop; deuteranopia (the critic measured green and coral converging there, so shape must keep
  carrying the difference: rings are always you).
