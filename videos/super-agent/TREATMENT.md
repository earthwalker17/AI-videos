# Treatment: Who Routes the Agents?

Three structurally different candidates, written independently by three design agents under
the treatment rule (no earlier film or template studied first), then scored by three judges
(clarity, fidelity to the source, craft) on 2026-11-08. A synthesis recommended a hybrid; the
user chose it the same day. Each candidate has its point of view, its governing image, the arc
in beats, the sound idea and what could go wrong. The chosen treatment, its art direction and
its sound follow under **Choice**.

## Candidate A
**Redrawing the Agent / 重画智能体.** The argument, drawn on one drafting sheet. 2:55.0
(5,250 frames: a 2-beat pickup plus 52 bars at 72 bpm).

- **Point of view.** An unseen systems architect at the drafting table, seen only through
  their tools (pen, compass, eraser shield, red pencil, stamp); the on-screen lines are their
  margin notes. The one human element is a box marked YOU · 你 inside a compass circle whose
  fixed radius is your attention. Every revision is judged by whether it fits that circle.
- **Governing image.** One engineering sheet in dark slate, with a border, registration marks
  and a title block (revision column, APPROVED field). Line grammar: blue construction line =
  being drafted or design only; white ink = built; red = stress and risk; dashed = hidden from
  the user; ghost = an abandoned revision.

**Arc**
1. **More sessions than attention (0:00–0:21.7).** The compass swings the attention circle
   round YOU; session boxes copy out 2, 4, 8, 16 and their hatches patch the circle; a
   dimension line reads 3–5. The title is stamped into the title block, and the source is
   named: "DeepSeek's Harness team proposed an answer: the Super-agent."
2. **REV A, one session for everything (0:21.7–0:35.0).** One long box, a step head advancing
   one token per beat, a queue at its port, task hatches blurring into moiré; red revision
   clouds.
3. **REV B, a fresh session per event (0:35.0–0:45.0).** A spray of boxes; two parts of one
   conversation land in different boxes; up to 12 reply lines flood the circle; red again.
4. **REV C, two levels (0:45.0–1:18.3).** A datum line; a compass-drawn Router above it, where
   a sketched three-way split is struck through; isolated Worker boxes below; one reply line to
   YOU, and the circle is re-inked white. A rest bar, a "built in the prototype" stamp, and
   the Workers turn into dashed hidden lines.
5. **Stress tests (1:18.3–1:45.0).** Red load arrows each get a drawn answer: a two-stage
   sieve, a batching bracket with 1 → 40 / min and 6.4 s → 1.6 s, a dashed Thread bypass lane
   (fewer Router decisions).
6. **The test (1:45.0–2:08.3).** A detail callout re-plots the synthetic test with no minute
   values: "~10%" and "≈ ½" dimensions, run squares for delegation, 2–4× the tokens.
7. **Always on, never inked (2:08.3–2:21.7).** A cloud band drawn only in blue construction
   line with machines below; a red intrusion runs down to every machine and leaves an open
   revision triangle.
8. **Sign-off and end card (2:21.7–2:55.0).** YOU and its circle rise to the APPROVED field and
   draw a check; the camera dollies into the title block, which becomes the end card.

**Sound.** Synthesised at 72 bpm with a two-beat pickup (25 frames per beat). D Dorian
resolving to D major with a Lydian colour at REV C; one motif, the A–D pickup, rising an octave
at sign-off. Felt piano for the motif, a low bowed pad for the Router, modal marimba Workers
(A, D, F♯), Karplus–Strong token ticks. REV A is a monophonic line smeared by a second motif a
semitone away; REV B a burst of plucks collapsing into a cluster; batched ticks release as one
chord on the downbeat; the bypass is a glissando over the pad; the intrusion is two beats of
silence and a low minor second.

**What could go wrong.** Five drafting conventions to decode in a film about attention; the
3–5 dimension line reads as a measured limit; "often didn't" is wrong (the unprompted session
never delegated); the always-on line used an idiom that can mean "in theory it works"; the
sieve, bypass lane and cloud-above/machines-below band echo three of the article's figures;
72 bpm is not frame-exact at eighths and sixteenths; deliberate moiré will crawl in the
platform re-encodes; drawn props (compass, eraser shield, stamp) are where a procedural picture
looks rough.

**Judges:** clarity 8 · fidelity 7 · craft 6.5 (total 21.5). The most retellable argument, but
a palette close to the genre look and three figures echoing the article.

## Candidate B
**From Noise to Sign-off / 从噪声到验收.** The life of one bug report on a warm-paper transit
map. 2:50.7 (5,120 frames: 64 bars at 90 bpm).

- **Point of view.** The protagonist is one invented event: a bug report drawn as a vermilion
  pill with a beetle glyph, followed from the noise to its sign-off. "You" are an ink ring: the
  switchboard in act one, then moved to the edge, where you only receive answers and sign off.
- **Governing image.** One route map on a warm-paper sheet, read like a transit map (lines at
  0/45/90°, stations, an interchange roundel). Act one is graphite pencil; at the turn the two
  failed sketches are inked into the Router and the Worker lines. Line style carries status:
  solid ink = built in the public prototype, dashed = proposed, pencil = improvised or open.
  Nothing drawn is removed, so the last wide shot is the summary map.

**Arc**
1. **Buried, and you are the switchboard (0:00–0:26.7).** The report sinks in a column of grey
   group-chat pills; the title sets as a map cartouche. Your ring drops it into a pencil session
   tile, then hops between five tiles that fade when left, leaving a pencil tangle. The 3–5
   figure is credited to the authors' experience: "You've become the switchboard."
2. **Two easy fixes (0:26.7–0:42.7).** Sketch A: one long track, queued and braided. Sketch B:
   one stub per message, each replying to you. "Continuity or speed: must we pick one?", then a
   half-bar breath.
3. **Two levels (0:42.7–1:14.7).** On the ink downbeat sketch A's track curls into the Router
   roundel and sketch B's stubs straighten into Worker lines; your ring glides to the edge. The
   report passes railway points and a gate (the two-stage filter), the Router (which never does
   or splits the work) and a Worker platform of its own; a built note follows.
4. **A follow-up, and a busy hour (1:14.7–1:33.3).** A follow-up rides an express flyover over
   the Router into the subscribed Worker (no faster, but fewer Router decisions); in a
   busy hour arrivals queue while the Router thinks and go in together, beside a stress plate.
5. **Back to you, then rest (1:33.3–1:49.3).** One answer comes back, your ring stamps it
   green, and the whole route is traced during a five-second rest.
6. **The evidence (1:49.3–2:10.7).** A timetable-style re-plot of the synthetic test with no
   minute values: the slowest tasks about half, typical ones about 10% better, the single
   session never delegated, about 3.8× the tokens.
7. **Still open (2:10.7–2:21.3).** A dashed boundary for always-on, machine nodes at the line
   ends, the laptop folding shut; a hijack spreads as hatching and leaves a pencil "?".
8. **Your attention (2:21.3–2:50.7).** The finished map at 0.5×, your path one short stroke at
   the edge; "the bottleneck is now your attention"; the end card.

**Sound.** A synthesised chamber score at 90 bpm (80 frames per bar) in D major, B minor for
the open problems: FM mallet, felt piano, a bowed pad, sine bass, paper-and-ink foley. The
report's rising fourth A4–D5 plays at each station and completes as A4–D5–F♯5 at sign-off. The
ensemble enters on the ink downbeat; each Worker adds a counter-melody (three at most);
pass-through skips the Router's chord; batching releases as one chord; the rest is a held Dmaj9.

**What could go wrong.** A noise-first hook frames the secondary mechanism, not the attention
problem; the payoff comes before the evidence; the filter interrupts the model; top-left
two-line captions sit in the danmaku band and run past 58 characters; vermilion and green at
similar lightness fail for deuteranopes; "Proposed next" reads as a roadmap; a dashed boundary
round built parts; no in-film credit before the end card; a rolling stress plate implies
measured intermediate values; the 3840×3200 world does not fit at 0.5×; pencil may read as
grey vector lines; a detuned-saw pad; the transit map is a familiar trope, and the channel
already has a map film.

**Judges:** clarity 7 · fidelity 8 · craft 8 (total 23). The best honesty and buildability, but
a noise-first hook and its payoff before the evidence.

## Candidate C
**The Attention Budget / 注意力预算.** Your attention as a ribbon of fixed width. 2:56.0
(5,280 frames: 66 bars at 90 bpm).

- **Point of view.** Second person. You are a flat ink head-and-shoulders silhouette seen from
  behind; the machines never get a face. The film follows only where your attention goes.
- **Governing image.** One vermilion ribbon from your head whose total width is fixed at 28 px
  (asserted in code). It splits into strands that always add up to 28 px, and unused attention
  coils back as a disc of the same area. Warm paper and ink; vermilion only for attention, moss
  only for "real"; danger as ink density, never a new hue.

**Arc**
1. **One ribbon, and you are the hidden router (0:00–0:32.0).** Frame 0 is already moving: the
   ribbon whips between five session cards, and each re-attach rescans the card. Trigger chips
   spawn 5, 8, 12 cards; strands thin and break past a soft "3-5" bracket; an ink ring closes
   round the head: "You have become the Router."
2. **Two obvious fixes (0:32.0–0:48.0).** Two benches split by a fold: one wide session card,
   serial and blurring; one card per event, fast, disconnected, all replying to you.
3. **The turn (0:48.0–1:12.0).** The ring lifts off the head and becomes the Router; the ribbon
   recoils into a still coil of saved attention; whole tasks go to Worker cards; one slip comes
   down to you; a built tag; the Workers fade toward pale.
4. **Why one Router keeps up (1:12.0–1:33.3).** A comb of ink teeth drops most chips; arrivals
   during a sweep stack and merge; the stress-test numbers; a Thread follow-up takes a dotted
   side lane past the Router.
5. **The test (1:33.3–1:52.0).** The Worker cards morph into chart rows on an axis to "30 min":
   never delegated unprompted, slipped 1 in 5 when told to, slowest tasks about half, about
   3.8× the tokens.
6. **Rest, then attention moves up (1:52.0–2:10.7).** A two-bar rest; the ribbon goes out at
   full width only to one decision card, and a moss tick stamps it.
7. **What it opens up (2:10.7–2:29.3).** A dashed compute band above a fold with machines
   below; a hijack runs down to every machine; credentials in an opaque sleeve; the ribbon
   reaches to step in and finds no handle.
8. **Bookend and credits (2:29.3–2:56.0).** The frame-0 framing; a card pings, the ping goes to
   the Router, the ribbon does not move: "Now, one is enough."

**Sound.** 90 bpm, 66 bars. Modal marimba for sessions and Workers, felt piano for you, a sine
bass for the Router's pulse, paper foley well under the music. The session motifs pile into a
quiet 3:4:5 polyrhythm that snaps into one D-major arpeggio at the turn; the two fixes are split
hard left and right; batching rings as one chord on the downbeat; chart rows sound for as long
as their values; a borrowed minor iv for the risks; a target of about −16 LUFS.

**What could go wrong.** The hardest build (a multi-strand variable-width ribbon with a coil);
strands under 3 px alias in the re-encode; 36 px type at the floor; a top-left register in the
danmaku band; the comb, side lane and machines-below-a-fold echo the article's figures; a
minute axis and p95 labels invite the "16 vs 29 minutes" misreading; p50 is drawn but not
narrated; the source is never named in the body; the open problems follow the conclusion, a
false ending; "Now, one is enough" claims success for a proposal; −16 LUFS departs from the
repo's −14; a silhouette risks looking like clip art.

**Judges:** clarity 7.5 · fidelity 5 · craft 7.5 (total 20). The best single image and the most
human hook, but weak attribution, minute values on the chart and the hardest build.

## Choice
**"Hybrid on B (Recommended)"**, chosen by the user on 2026-11-08: *Who Routes the Agents? /
谁来调度智能体？*. This is the treatment approval (gate `treatment`), recorded in `video.json`
in these words. The same day the user chose the look **"Dark ink map (Recommended)"**, the
narration **"Text-led, no voice (Recommended)"**, and **"Connect Chrome"** for a frame study of
比特新语, made in the user's browser. The title is a working title.

### Why
Judges' totals: **B 23** (clarity 7, fidelity 8, craft 8), **A 21.5** (8, 7, 6.5), **C 20**
(7.5, 5, 7.5). B won the two lenses that decide whether a three-minute text-led explainer is
both honest and buildable on the reference host. Its clarity losses were problems of order,
not of the image, so they could be fixed without changing it.

- **From B, the base:** the one governing image (a route map on which each failed fix becomes
  half of the right one), the honesty (line style carries built, proposed and open; no minutes
  on the chart; "not faster tasks"; machines at the line ends), and geometry that can be
  generated rather than drawn.
- **From A, the argument order, not its sheet:** an attention-first hook; the source credited
  before "the authors' experience"; the model complete before any mechanism; the struck-through
  split at the Router; the evidence opened by its caveat; the risk before the conclusion;
  sign-off as the last idea; lower-left one-line notes clear of danmaku. A's dark drafting
  sheet was not used: it scored lowest on craft, sat close to the genre palette and echoed three
  of the article's figures.
- **From C, one gesture:** the ring that closes round you, then lifts off and becomes the
  Router (an interchange is a ring, so it is native to B's map). Also the exact frame-0
  bookend, and in sound the polyrhythm that collapses at the turn and chart lanes that sound
  for as long as their values. C's ribbon was not used: fidelity scored C 5, and it is the
  hardest build.

**Judges' fixes applied.**
- Fidelity: credit in the film at 0:06.7; "Proposed:" plus a "not in the public prototype"
  tag, dashed only on new parts; 2–4× named against both baselines under a cache-read token
  header; the stress test as endpoints only, "avg", mock Workers; the built tag scoped to the
  two levels; the request an illustration under "By design" with the off-by-default tag;
  "about 3–5" with no threshold line; no minutes on the chart and p50 narrated; "never
  delegated"; "work requests"; "stronger baseline"; "hears every channel"; tasks handed over
  whole; a conditional conclusion; sign-off on a delivered result; an ending that claims
  intent, not success; zh "可能" and "相连"; no sieve, bypass lane, cloud-above/machines-below
  or hub-and-spoke form.
- Clarity: the attention-first hook; the model before the filter; the batching result said in
  words; cause before consequence; an explicit bookend; lower-left, one line per language.
- Craft: measured line caps; cues on the eighth-note grid; a compact 3840×2160 world, so 0.5×
  shows everything; vermilion and green separated by lightness and form; textured, tapered
  sketch strokes; an additive pad instead of saws; the roundel and cartouche as signature;
  −14 LUFS.

**The look.** The synthesis proposed a light, warm "survey sheet". The user chose the dark ink
map instead: a dark tinted ground with flat bright ink lines and no glow, bloom, particles or
serif. The film differs from the genre through line grammar and semantic colour, not through
its background. Only the palette and act one's medium change (the synthesis's graphite pencil
becomes dim, tapered sketch lines on the dark ground); the rest of the synthesis's grammar
stands. See **Art direction** below.

### The chosen treatment: *Who Routes the Agents? / 谁来调度智能体？*

**Logline.** Your AI agents work in parallel and you can't, so you have quietly become their
router. On one route map, a request that sank in the noise shows why the two obvious fixes
fail. It then shows how the Super-agent design takes that job off you: a long-lived Router
above isolated Workers. The film also covers what the authors' synthetic test does and does not
show, and what is still unsolved, until all that is left for you is signing off.

**Point of view.** "You" are a small ink ring on the map. In act one you are the hub, the
switchboard. At the turn the router role lifts off you. From then on your ring never moves
again; it only receives answers and gives sign-offs. The vermilion request is the camera's
tracer, and it answers one question: who carries this now? It is not the narrator; it is the
thing you could not carry.

**Governing image.** One dark ink map, a world of 3840×2160 under one camera that only
translates and scales, drawn and read like a transit map: lines at 0/45/90°, stations and an
interchange ring. It changes in four steps.
1. **Act one is sketch line:** dim, tapered strokes, improvised and held together by you:
   session tiles round your ring, and your switching trail as a tangle.
2. **The turn is ink.** The ring around you lifts off. Sketch A's single long track curls into
   it to become the Router, and keeps its continuity as a memory track of ticks. Sketch B's
   stubs straighten into Worker branch lines, which keeps the parallelism. Each wrong answer
   turns out to be half of the right one.
3. **The request then travels the inked route:** railway points and a gate (the filter), the
   Router, its own Worker platform, and a flyover over the Router for Thread follow-ups.
4. **Line style carries how real each part is:** solid ink = in the public research prototype;
   dashed ink = proposed, used only on new parts and never as a boundary round built ones;
   sketch = improvised or open. A sketch "?" stays for good.

Nothing drawn is removed, so the final wide shot is the summary map; the act-one tangle stays
as a ghost under the Router.

**Grid.** 90 bpm in 4/4: 80 frames per bar, 20 per beat, 10 per eighth. 66 bars = 5,280
frames = **2:56.0**. Every story beat starts on a downbeat (bar *n* starts at frame 80(*n* − 1)).
Cues start on eighths; each has a 6-frame wipe in and a 6-frame fade out, and its unchanged
hold is at least max(1.8 s, reading time + 0.6 s) + 0.1 s in both languages. The margins were
checked by script; the smallest is 0.11 s. The zh lines follow the Netflix zh-Hans convention
in [the subtitles reference](../../references/subtitles.md): no commas or periods, a space
marks a pause, and ： and ？ are full-width. Cue numbers C1–C28 count the narration lines.

## Art direction: dark ink map
A dark tinted ground with flat bright ink lines: no glow, bloom, blur, particles, 3D, serif or
vignette. The film differs from the genre through line grammar and semantic colour, not through
the background. These are starting values, to be proven on the lookdev stills.

**Palette.** Nothing else is used. Contrast ratios are against the ground.

| Role | Hex | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Ground: flat; the centre may lift, the corners never darken | `#202226` | BT.709 limited luma Y 45.1, above QA's black threshold 37.9 |
| Panel: cartouche, timetable | `#282A2F` | |
| Dot grid: 2.5 px dots at a 96 world-px pitch, faded out below 0.7× zoom (no moiré) | `#2E3035` | |
| Ink: built structure, EN text | `#E8E6E0` | 12.8:1 |
| zh line | `#B9B7B1` | 7.9:1 |
| Sketch: act one, chart rails, open items; ghosts at 12% | `#75736D` | 3.4:1 |
| Message pill fill (sketch-tone outline and dashes) | `#3B3D42` | |
| Receded: Workers once stepped down to "implementation detail" | `#55575C` | |
| Vermilion: only the one request, its route, its thread and its follow-up; never text | `#E8603F` | L* 58 |
| Sign-off green: only your sign-off and the return route, which is also a cased line | `#8FD3A8` | L* 79 |

- **Colour-blind safety.** Green and vermilion differ by lightness (ΔL* 21) and by form: the
  return route is a cased double line with hollow reply pills and a tick, so the out-and-back
  route reads under deuteranopia and protanopia.
- **Risk is hatching, never a new hue:** 45° ink hatching, 2 px strokes at an 8 px pitch.
- **No blue anywhere** (blue is the article's accent).
- **A static, seeded dither tile** prevents banding in the dark ground. It is baked once, never
  animated.

**Black-detector margin.** QA runs ffmpeg blackdetect with `pix_th=0.10` (a frame counts when
98% of its pixels fall under the threshold, for at least 0.5 s). On BT.709 limited range that
threshold is luma 37.9; the ground is 45.1, a margin of about 7 codes. Therefore:
- every full-frame transition dissolves to the ground, never to black;
- nothing in the palette is darker than the ground;
- the ground stays flat, with no vignette: the centre may lift, the corners never darken;
- neither the grade nor the dither may lower the ground.

**Type.** All OFL, loaded through fontsource and the kit's font loader with coverage proof,
awaited before frame 0; Noto Sans SC is subset to the glyphs actually used.

There are no boundaries round built parts. The topology is a trunk with branches, never a
radial spoke diagram.

**Motion grammar**
1. **Grid.** Everything sits on the 90 bpm grid: structural changes on downbeats, small events
   on beats or eighths.
2. **Camera.** Translate and scale only, 0.5×–1.7×. Moves start on downbeats, last exactly 1
   or 2 bars, use cubic in-out and stay under about 600 px/s on screen. One move at a time; no
   rotation, shake or cuts, except the dissolve to the ground before the end card.
3. **Ink is drawn, never faded in.** It travels along its path at about 1000 px/s on screen,
   with a 4-frame ease in and a 6-frame ease out.
4. **Sketch lines.** Tapered, variable-width stroke polygons with seeded pressure and a seeded
   scribble pass, in the sketch tone; any grain is a baked, seeded texture clipped to the
   stroke, never a filter. Erasing leaves a 12% ghost.
5. **Tokens.** They hop station to station in one beat (sine in-out) or ride lines at constant
   speed, arriving on eighths. They never teleport.
6. **Emphasis.** One 2 px ink ring that expands and fades over 16 frames.
7. **Step-down.** Over one bar.
8. **Text.** A 6-frame left-to-right wipe, EN and zh together, then a 6-frame fade out. Never a
   typewriter reveal.
9. **Never used:** glow, bloom, blur, particles, light leaks, lens effects, 3D or parallax.

**What never moves:** the ground and the dot grid (except with the camera), the notes block,
the cartouche, the Router after the turn, and your ring after the turn. That last rule is part
of the thesis.

**Why it is distinct from the genre.** The genre was studied in a frame study of 比特新语 on
2026-11-08 (mostly at 360p; font names judged by eye), recorded in
[the style reference](../../references/vibe-knowledge-style-2026-11.md).
- A flat, only slightly cool dark grey with no vignette, nebula or bloom, instead of a
  hue-tinted near-black (about `#020408`–`#06080d` as measured in two of the films) with a
  lifted centre and a glowing focal element.
- Flat ink and sketch lines whose style carries status, instead of thin glowing strokes,
  luminous dots and particle swarms.
- Two semantic colours, each with one meaning and never used for text, instead of one accent
  shared by the diagram and a glowing caption keyword.
- Lower-left sans notes, one line per language, instead of a centred serif caption band near
  the bottom.
- A sans map cartouche instead of a glowing heavy-serif title lockup.
- One continuous camera and dissolves to the ground, instead of white flashes or fog washes at

## Sound design
**Tempo and clock.** 90 bpm in 4/4: 80 frames per bar, 20 per beat, 10 per eighth, 5 per
sixteenth. At 48 kHz that is 1,600 samples per frame, so audio and picture are exact. Picture
and score read one typed timeline module (bar map, cue table, event schedule).

**Key and harmony.** D Dorian for act one; D major from the turn (F♮ to F♯), with Lydian colour
allowed; B minor and a borrowed iv over a drone for the open problem; D major add9 to close.

**Leitmotif.** The request's rising fourth, A4 to D5. In the hook a following F♮5 falls away
unfinished; it recurs at each station; it completes as A4, D5, F♯5 at sign-off; in the bookend
it plays once without any felt-piano answer; it is the solo under the end card.

**Instruments.** All deterministic synthesis, no samples.

| Voice | Synthesis | Stands for |
|---|---|---|
| FM mallet | Sine carrier, 1:4 modulator, fast decay | Messages and the motif |
| Modal marimba | 3–5 inharmonic partials, exponential decay | Session tiles, then Workers (A, D, F♯) |
| Felt piano | Additive, soft hammer noise, low-pass | You |
| Sine bass | Light saturation | The Router's pulse, from the turn |
| Bowed pad | Additive, slow-attack partial stacks, gentle vibrato; no detuned saws | Continuity, from the turn |
| Wood ticks | Karplus–Strong | Message pills |
| Foley | Filtered noise | Sketch and ink strokes, points clack, gate bell, stamp thump, pop, lid click |

**How cues lock to bars.** Camera moves and structural changes land on downbeats. The rules
that make the story audible:
- act-one hops speed up from quarters to sixteenths, and the tile motifs build a 3:4:5
  polyrhythm;
- the two sketches are split hard left and right;
- at the turn everything collapses into one unison arpeggio on the bar-17 downbeat;
- batched ticks are released as one chord on the next downbeat;
- a Thread follow-up skips the Router's chord;
- chart lanes sound for a time proportional to their value.

**Rests and silences,** all declared in the cue sheet so QA measures them: bar 16, beats 3–4
(the breath); bar 27 (pad and two plucks); bar 48, beats 1–2 (silence before the intrusion);
bars 51–52 (held Dmaj9 and the scratching drag, 5.3 s); f5260–5280 (decay). The end-card hold,
f4720–5260, is declared as a hold span so that freezedetect allows it.

**UI sound palette.** Sketch scratch, ink click, a soft brush for the noise sink, points clack,
gate bell, mechanical detent, soft pop, felt stamp thump, lid click. All sit 12–18 dB under the
music bed, with no whoosh risers, beat drops or sub hits.

**Master.** −14 LUFS integrated (±1), true peak at most −1 dBTP, through the repo's two-pass
loudnorm. Before the score is built, the generic DSP, reverb, mastering and mixer code will be
copied from the frozen halloween-gift slot into a shared score kit at
`packages/pipeline/src/audio/runtime/kit/`, which then gains the voices this film needs
(PolyBLEP oscillators, ADSR, modal and FM voices, an additive pad, an arpeggiator, delay,
ducking, the UI-sound palette). The frozen film is not touched and keeps its own copy at its
tag.

**Listening gate.** Agents cannot hear the score, so the user listens: a score sketch comes with
the lookdev stills, and the full score before the review cut.
