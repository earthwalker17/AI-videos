# V1, archived: what it was and what it taught

V1 of *Who Routes the Agents?* was finished on 2026-11-15 and archived unpublished the same day.
It is complete and rebuildable at the local tag `video/super-agent/v1-archived` (commit `b233c67`).
This page is the short record. It is not a template for V2: V2 may change the narrative, the look,
the engine, the length and the sound.

## Why it was archived
The review cut was approved, and the final was rendered and passed QA. Then the user decided not to
publish it: "Claude Fable 5.2 is out, and I've come across other videos on similar topics made with
it that look much better than ours." The user named the look. They did not comment on the facts or
the structure, and they said V2 may change "the narrative way and visual direction".
Shortly before, in the last critic round, a context-free reviewer agent had judged it the same
way, in its own words: "a handsome animated system diagram rather than a top-tier cinematic
explainer".

## What V1 was
- **Runtime and timing:** 3:44.0, which is 6,720 frames at 30 fps, or 84 bars of 4/4 at 90 bpm, so
  each bar was 80 frames. One cue sheet drove the picture and the score.
- **Text:** text-led, with no voice. There were 36 English lines in a block at the lower left, plus
  short bilingual tags. The zh captions were dropped on 2026-11-13, and the zh on long plates on
  2026-11-14.
- **Picture:** Remotion, with a lit WebGL world (React Three Fiber) under DOM text, posed by one
  camera function, with no cuts. Look round 1, a flat "dark ink map" on transit-map grammar, was
  rejected on 2026-11-08. Round 2, "the Register Desk", was signed off on 2026-11-10:
  - a slate desk;
  - parts in paper, enamel, graphite and vellum, where material meant status;
  - light only on live processes, with bloom 0.3;
  - a 15° long-lens tilt.
- **Sound:** a score synthesised in code, mastered to -14 LUFS.
- **Production:** seven sessions, from 2026-11-08 to 2026-11-15. The final's frames and score
  equalled the approved review cut's (frame hashes and audio samples), without its 0.5 s test card.
- **Title:** *Who Routes the Agents?* / 谁来调度智能体？, chosen with the treatment.

### The narrative
The spine was the four chores the viewer does as the "human router", handed over one by one. The
through-line was continuity against speed. A vermilion request ran through every mechanism as a
tracer.

| Part | Time | Content |
|---|---|---|
| The problem | 0:00–0:48 (21%) | Hook ("Your AI agents work in parallel. You can't."), title and credit; you have quietly become your sessions' router (3–5 sessions is the authors' experience); two easy fixes, one session or one per event, each half right |
| How it works | 0:48–2:11 | The turn: a long-lived Router that hears every channel, hands whole tasks over and never works, over isolated Workers that report up; events, not prompts (two-stage filter, pickup off by default); how one Router keeps up (batching, context as a cache, recall by ID); waiting handed over (pass-through, background jobs, 100-word replies) |
| Evidence | 2:11–2:32 (9.5%) | The synthetic test, caveat first: the slowest tasks take about half the time; typical tasks are about 10% faster than the stronger baseline; the single session never delegated; 2–4× the tokens |
| Proposed and open | 2:32–3:01 | Keeping it running (the cloud loop, the Watchdog); still open (a hijacked loop, sharding, stepping in midway) |
| Meaning and close | 3:01–3:44 (19%) | You sign off the result; six classic systems ideas named; attention becomes the limit; "By design, you don't have to."; the end card |

The mechanism share was about 43% of the runtime: 36 of 84 bars, counting "How it works" and
"keeping it running". That was up from 14% in the 2:56 cut, which the user had judged too
shallow.

### Directions tried
These are the directions judged, with their scores. They are not options to pick again unless V2
chooses to.
- **Treatments (session 1):** a journey on a route map (B, 23), an argument (A, 21.5) and an
  attention study (C, 20). The user took a hybrid on B.
- **Round-2 looks, out of 30:**
  - "the Register Desk", a desk of paper and enamel parts (20.0, chosen);
  - "Lamp & Current", a lit network (18.0);
  - "Lamp & Enamel", an instrument (17.0).
- **Rejected outright:**
  - conduits everywhere with blooming pulses, which was a node-link graph again and close to the
    genre's glowing lines;
  - a felt figure and a 34° diorama, a risk of kitsch or a product-render sheen;
  - a radial act one, which echoes the article's hub-and-spoke figure.
- **A shared error:** all three round-2 designs drew the Router with no tool at all. The article
  allows a Router with no Bash tool (block 31), but the prototype's Router keeps a restricted shell,
  and V1 showed the two levels as built. The source is the session-3 design synthesis, which was not
  kept.

## What worked
- **Honesty as form.** Built, proposed and open each had their own line and material, with a key.
  Each status tag was scoped to exactly the lines it was true for, and tests enforced it. The
  context-free viewer found the film "honest about what is built and what is proposed".
- **A question for every mechanism.** Each mechanism answered "which of your chores did this take
  over?". The context-free viewer "took away the argument".
- **Misreadings guarded on screen.** Grey counterfactual cards showed the Router splitting a task,
  the Router doing the work, and "faster tasks", then struck them out.
- **One event list.** Every effect was a pure sum over the cue sheet's events. The same lists placed
  the score's hits. 639 tests in the video, among them the score's sync tests, held picture and
  sound together.
- **The score.** The user called it "Right direction" twice. What made it:
  - deterministic synthesis;
  - a motif left incomplete until the sign-off;
  - structure made audible: a polyrhythm for overload, a hard left/right split for the two fixes,
    a unison at the turn, batched ticks released as one chord;
  - UI sounds 12–18 dB under the bed, and no whooshes.
- **The process.**
  - The lookdev was built in the film's own frame components, so the film drew the signed-off
    frames.
  - A/B style frames let the user choose from images.
  - Whole-film critic rounds used span critics and a context-free viewer, and sent every serious
    finding to a verifier told to refute it. About a fifth were downgraded or refuted.
  - Fix lanes worked on disjoint files.

## What did not work
- **The look, twice.** Round 1 was flat outline geometry, which the user called "too simple and
  crude to be something worth watching". Round 2 was richer and was signed off, but the finished
  film still read as a diagram, not cinema:
  - the wide shots filled a central band;
  - much of the in-world text looked readable but was not, at 540p;
  - the best stretch was a close-up, where the picture carried the idea.

  Round 2 retired the transit-map grammar: the first five round-2 strands had kept it, and a
  session-3 critic traced the crudeness to that metaphor. But the Register Desk kept a node-and-link
  system diagram as its governing image, a Router above bays joined by cords and flights. That is
  how the viewer read it. This is hindsight, and the critic's notes were not kept.
- **The critic's bar.** The critic was calibrated only to fail what the user had rejected. It
  scored the signed-off look at 23–30 of 38, and a pass never meant "competitive with the best
  current films".
- **Reading-bound pacing.** Text was on screen about 81% of the time before the end card. The
  typical gap between lines was about 1.3 s, against an aim of one bar of picture-only time per
  mechanism. Every line met the
  reading hold, and still the turn and the idea labels felt rushed, while the act-one desk, the
  wide map and the end card dragged.
- **Bilingual effort.** The zh copy was written and signed off twice, then dropped once the film
  became YouTube only. Long bilingual plates had doubled the reading load. Short tags such as
  "you · 你" added character.
- **Copy caught late.**
  - Percentages were inverted: "30% fewer decisions" should have been about 23%.
  - Absolute words had to be qualified: "All traffic" became "Every task", "Unprompted" became
    "Left to choose", and "talks to you" became "answers you".
- **Drawing ahead of the words.** Thread cords drawn before the line that explains them read as
  bypassing the Router.
- **Sessions.**
  - The plan grew from 4 sessions to 8, and 7 were used.
  - Three sessions were interrupted four times: by accident, by a usage limit, by a reboot, and by
    low memory under heavy context pressure. Only the last is in the repository's record; the
    others are from the session transcripts, read 2026-11-15.
  - SCRIPT.md and STORYBOARD.md grew into frame-level build logs, and the README into 680 lines.

## Lessons, and where they now live
- **Benchmark early, and again before the final.** Put frames beside the newest films the user names,
  side by side, at lookdev and again before the final. When a look is rejected, reopen the governing
  image, not only its grammar and rendering. Recorded in [PRODUCTION.md](../../docs/PRODUCTION.md) and in the
  critic's calibration ([frame-critic.md](../../.claude/agents/frame-critic.md)).
- **Explainer craft.**
  - Audit the share of the runtime that explains how it works.
  - Give each mechanism a question the viewer already holds.
  - Treat holds as a floor, not a pace.
  - Budget picture-only time.
  - Draw no link before its line.
  - Scope status labels, and check absolute words.

  Recorded in [SERIES.md](../../series/science-explainers/SERIES.md).
- **Numbers.** Recompute every on-screen number from the source's figures, name the baseline, and
  show no units the source cannot back. Recorded in [PRODUCTION.md](../../docs/PRODUCTION.md) (the
  facts bar) and in [FACTS.md](FACTS.md), section 6.
- **Platform and languages.** Settle the platform and the languages at the brief. They drive the
  reading budget and the copy work.
- **Documents.** Keep SCRIPT.md to copy and intent, and STORYBOARD.md to shot intent. Build history
  belongs in git.
- **Sessions.** Size each session so that it finishes with its own critic round, and write durable
  handoffs. Check for a pending reboot and for free memory before long workflows.
- **Engine and audio.** These lessons are in the knowledge cards:
  - [r3f-under-remotion.md](../../knowledge/r3f-under-remotion.md): the lit world's pitfalls;
  - [dom-text-determinism.md](../../knowledge/dom-text-determinism.md);
  - [loudness-two-pass.md](../../knowledge/loudness-two-pass.md): the AAC true-peak margin;
  - [camera-moves-by-screen-flow.md](../../knowledge/camera-moves-by-screen-flow.md);
  - the measured render costs in [ENGINE.md](../../engines/remotion/ENGINE.md).

## Taking something from V1
Read a file with `git show video/super-agent/v1-archived:videos/super-agent/<path>`. Rebuild the
film from a worktree at the tag (docs/PRODUCTION.md, "Rebuild a frozen video").

Take only what V2 actually chooses to use. A generic piece goes to `packages/` first, with V1
counted as its first user. Film-specific code is rewritten, not copied back. The 2D kit's diagram
grammar (`camera`, `diagram`, `sketch`) belongs to the round-1 look the user rejected.

| Path at the tag | What it is |
|---|---|
| `src/cues.ts`, `src/cues.test.ts` | The cue sheet: bars, narration, schedules read by picture and score, and 13 rule tests (holds, eighth-grid starts, no overlaps, line widths, zh style, hits on beats) |
| `src/kit/timeline.ts`, `src/kit/text.tsx` | The musical grid and progress helpers; text reveals (a 6-frame wipe, never a typewriter), the notes block, `GREY_TEXT`, explicit zh breaks |
| `src/kit/dither.tsx`, `src/look/slices.ts` | Seeded dither through `<Img>`; `planSlices`, which picks the fontsource CJK slices that a film's strings need |
| `src/desk/camera.ts` | One pure camera that poses both the WebGL world and the DOM labels (1 unit = 1 px at zoom 1) |
| `src/desk/gl/{light,post,veil}.ts`, `src/desk/{footprints,pools}.ts` | Analytic light: footprint shadows, light pools, `PreToeEffect` for three's neutral tone mapping, a dissolve to the bare ground |
| `audio/instruments.ts`, `audio/foley.ts`, `audio/score.test.ts` | 14 synthesised voices, 23 UI sounds, and score tests (mix holes, rests, onsets) |
| `src/lookdev/` | Probe and style-frame entries (`round2.tsx`, A/B angle and bloom); `stills.tsx` holds the rejected round-1 stills (first at commit `9a4a565`) |
| `src/thumbnail/` | A thumbnail rendered from the film's world, with a canvas-drawn title |
| `TREATMENT.md`, `SCRIPT.md`, `STORYBOARD.md`, `README.md` | V1's full treatment, copy (EN and the signed-off zh), shots and session records |
